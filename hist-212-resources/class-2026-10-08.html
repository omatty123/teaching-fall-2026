People are the root. People are the water.

People are the Root

Jia Yi · today’s reading

“It is said that in government, the people are in every way the root (base) [ben]. For the state, the ruler, and the officials, the people constitute the root. Thus the security of the state or its endangerment depends on them [the people]; the prestige of the ruler or his disgrace depends on them; and the honor of the officials or their debasement is contingent on them.”

Jia Yi, New Writings, “Dazheng” · SCT 291. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.8

The ruler depends on the people. Their support determines whether his rule is secure.

People as Water

Xunzi · return to October 1

“The lord is the boat. The common people are the water. The water can support the boat. The water can also overturn the boat.”
“Thus, if the lord of men wishes to be secure, then nothing works better than governing evenhandedly and showing concern for the people.”

Xunzi, ch.9, “The Rule of a True King,” 9.88–101 · Eric L. Hutton, Xunzi: The Complete Text, p.70. Oct 1 reader · PDF p.2

The people can sustain or overturn a ruler. Xunzi connects his security to how he treats them.

The ruler works for the people

Dong Zhongshu · today’s reading

“The king likewise constantly takes loving and benefiting all-under-Heaven as his intent and takes bringing peace and joy to an age as his task.”
“Therefore one who acts as king is no more than Heaven’s agent.”

Dong Zhongshu, “The Way of the King Penetrates Three” · SCT 301 · Sarah A. Queen. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.11

Government has a task: benefit the people. “The ruler works for the people” summarizes Dong’s claim; the quoted wording gives his stated duty.

Who depends on whom? Compare the people’s power in Jia Yi and Xunzi with the ruler’s duty in Dong Zhongshu. Then read Li Si below: whose pleasure is government supposed to serve?

Set against Li Si: whom does the state serve?

Li Si · The ruler’s pleasure

“Thus the ruler will by himself control the empire and will not be controlled by anyone. Then he can enjoy himself to the utmost.”

“Now, if a ruler will not practice the intelligent methods of Shen Buhai and Han Feizi, or apply the system of censure in order to utilize the empire for his own pleasure, but on the contrary purposelessly tortures his body and wastes his mind in devotion to the people — then he becomes the slave of the common people instead of the domesticator of the empire.”

“Memorial on Exercising Heavy Censure,” SCT 211 · Burton Watson. Oct 6 reader · PDF p.20

Dong Zhongshu · The king’s duty

“Therefore one who acts as king is no more than Heaven’s agent.”

“The king likewise constantly takes loving and benefiting all-under-Heaven as his intent and takes bringing peace and joy to an age as his task.”

SCT 301 · Sarah A. Queen. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.11

Censure here means inspection and punishment (SCT 210, note 10).

Identify the beneficiary of government in each passage. What obligation does Dong place on the king?

Lord Shang: thirteen numbers

What a strong state must know

“A strong state should know thirteen numbers within its borders: the number of granaries and residents; the number of adult men and women; the number of the old and infirm; the number of officials and men-of-service; the number of those who obtain emoluments by talking; the number of beneficial people; the number of horses, oxen, hay, and straw.”

The Book of Lord Shang §4.10 · Yuri Pines (2017), pp.154–155. Oct 1 reader · PDF pp.11–12

Emoluments are payments or benefits received for holding a position.

The categories in the passage

  1. Granaries
  2. Residents
  3. Adult men
  4. Women
  5. The old and infirm
  6. Officials
  7. Men-of-service
  8. Those paid for talking
  9. Beneficial people
  10. Horses
  11. Oxen
  12. Hay
  13. Straw

Reading aid: grouped from the quotation; item 8 is paraphrased.

Choose one category. Explain how knowing its number could help a ruler organize agriculture or war. Why count people who are paid for talking?

Lu Jia and Jia Yi: lessons from Qin

Lu Jia · What destroyed Qin?

“Duke Huan of Qi claimed hegemony through virtue, while the Second Emperor of the Qin perished through his fondness for penal codes.”

SCT 288. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.5

Jia Yi · The people’s strength

“Thus the people are the root of [dynastic] longevity, never to be taken lightly. … Moreover, the people have great strength and cannot be opposed. Oh, be warned, in any opposition to the people, the people will win.”

SCT 292. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.9

Compare Lu Jia with Li Si above. What Li Si treats as a method of secure rule, Lu Jia treats as a cause of failure. What evidence does each passage ask us to accept?

Dong Zhongshu: Heaven and the king

What the king follows

“He models himself on Heaven’s numerical categories and initiates affairs. He models himself on Heaven’s Way and sends forth his standards. He models himself on Heaven’s will and always returns to humaneness.”

SCT 301 · Sarah A. Queen. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.11

Compare these numerical categories with Lord Shang’s administrative counts. What is the ruler trying to understand in each?

How the king is corrected

“Portents are Heaven’s warnings; anomalies are Heaven’s threats. If Heaven warns [the ruler] and he does not acknowledge [these warnings], then Heaven sends anomalies to awe him.”

SCT 305 · adapted by Sarah A. Queen from Burton Watson. Oct 8 reader · PDF p.12

Portents and anomalies are unusual occurrences interpreted here as warnings about faults in government.

If an official interprets a portent as a warning, what can the official ask the emperor to change?
