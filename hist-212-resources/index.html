Legge’s translations use older spellings (Yü = Yu; Thang = Tang; Kâu = Zhou). The Shiji and Lienüzhuan are Han-period works describing earlier events; Sima Zhen’s supplement is later still. Text collections may contain layers of different dates and authorship.

Historical notes and sources

The chart’s names and date conventions are preserved, with approximate dates marked c. There is no year 0 in historical BCE/CE notation. Western Zhou ends in 771 BCE; Eastern Zhou conventionally begins in 770. King You ends Western Zhou, not the entire dynasty.

Xia’s chronology and Erlitou’s identification with it are disputed. Laozi’s sixth-century date is traditional and uncertain. The books contain material from different periods; their positions do not give exact composition dates. Shiji belongs to the Han period, around 100 BCE.

King Zhòu 紂 of Shang is different from the Zhōu 周 dynasty. The Zhou founders’ roles differ: Wen laid foundations, Wu led the conquest, and the Duke of Zhou consolidated the new order. City names around Luoyang have complex historical relationships.
