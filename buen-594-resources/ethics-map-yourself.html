Map Yourself

One question can’t tell you much about your ethics. Sixteen situations and a look at your own past year can show where you sit on the matrix, how strongly, how steadily, and what moves you.

  • Sixteen situationsEach has two questions. Choose a circle. The larger the circle, the more that side outweighs the other. The small middle circle means both weigh equally.
  • Your past yearSixteen statements about what you did and who bore the cost. Say how often each has been true of you.
  • Your mapYour place on the matrix, who and what shifts you, and how each type has shown up in you at its best and when it harms.

Answer for what you would do, which may differ from what you think you should do. Your answers stay in this browser and are sent to no one. This is a tool for class discussion and has not been tested as a psychological measure.

Your map

    When you shift

    The sixteen situations differ in four ways. The first card splits them by who the other person is, four situations each. The other three compare eight situations with the other eight. The farther apart the marks, the more that condition moves you.

    At its best, when it harms

    Every type on the matrix has helped people and has hurt people. One way to tell the two apart is to ask who paid. These bars come from your answers about the past year.

    A question for each type

    Each type protects something. Each has a question that catches it before it harms.
