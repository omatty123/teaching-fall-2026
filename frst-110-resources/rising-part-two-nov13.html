The tribe’s plan to bring families back together

The Jean Charles Choctaw Nation’s plan included people who had already lost their homes and left the island. Under Louisiana’s program, families displaced before Hurricane Isaac in 2012 could receive a lot but had to finance the house. In May 2026, the Nation said it was still working to reunite those families on higher ground. The tribal council’s plan · Louisiana’s eligibility rules · The Nation’s 2026 statement

Oregon’s bird observation station

“Connecting the Dots,” p. 185. Rush opens the next chapter with a rufous hummingbird in Oregon’s H. J. Andrews Experimental Forest. A bird that breeds in the mountains depends on places to feed along its migration route. Losing one of those places matters hundreds of miles away. On pp. 194–200, fieldworker Bryan Doyle takes Rush to two of his bird-count points, 400 and 40. Along the way she learns to recognize the song of a Swainson’s thrush. Read Rush’s opening · The forest’s account of her visit

After the 2023 fire. The 2023 Lookout Fire burned across about two-thirds of the experimental forest. In 2024 the bird crew returned to the burned slopes. Researchers still stand at fixed points for ten minutes and count the birds they see or hear. They also installed sound recorders at all 183 survey sites to track when birds arrive and leave. The survey log records visits to both of Rush’s points on June 23, 2025. That season, crews heard or saw Swainson’s thrush at both. Fire map · See the crew on the burned slopes · Bird counts and survey method

The owls at point 40 · pp. 200–205

When Rush brings Steve Ackers her photograph, he asks whether the birds might be barred owls. The two species compete for prey and habitat. A study published in 2026 used recordings from 1,027 survey areas across Oregon, Washington and northwestern California. Researchers detected northern spotted owls in 261 areas and barred owls in 891. The study describes the regional change. The fate of the particular owls Rush photographed is unknown. Oregon State’s account of the owl study

Rufous hummingbird numbers across North America

Across its North American breeding range, researchers estimated a 65% population decline from 1970 to 2019. That decline was already visible before the Lookout Fire. A morning’s count tells us which birds a crew detected at one spot. Estimating a population trend takes repeated counts from across the bird’s range. Read the hummingbird study
