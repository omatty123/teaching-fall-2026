Equiano and Omar ibn Said

Two autobiographical works · FRST 110 · October 30, 2026

Both authors write about enslavement and religious life. They write in different languages, for different audiences, and from different positions of freedom.

The works and their authors

1789 · English · Published book

Olaudah Equiano

The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano, or Gustavus Vassa, the African. Written by Himself.

Published in London after he had purchased his freedom. Its dedication addresses the British Parliament and the slave-trade debate.

1831 · Arabic · Handwritten autobiography

Omar ibn Said

Autobiography, also cataloged as The Life of Omar ben Saeed.

Written in North Carolina while he remained enslaved. Arabic learning and Qur’anic passages shape the account.

Translation is part of the comparison. Equiano composed his book in English; the English words attributed to Omar pass through a translator. Identify the translator and edition when quoting him.

Sources and limits

The comparison concerns the works as a whole; it is not limited to Equiano’s chapters III–IV. Descriptions of a text’s strategy are interpretations supported by the cited passages. They do not establish an author’s private motives.
