Three Pillars of Crafting Personas

Patricia Smith · “Buried,” pp. 63–64

Persona: a voice a writer creates to speak as someone or something else.

Instructions

  1. Open your book to “Buried,” pp. 63–64.
  2. Notice strengths and weaknesses in how Smith creates the father. Tie each observation to a specific detail in the text.
  3. Use those observations to craft your own three pillars of effective persona creation: principles for writing as someone—or something—else.
  4. Work by hand. Use lists, bullet points, notes, prose, crossings-out, and revisions. Show your thinking as it develops.

Example · working notes

One possible route through the task. Develop your own pillars.

“Plunge. Push. Lift. Toss it.”

  • starts with the body doing something
  • heavy shovel, swollen fingers, stopping for tea
  • make the character realistic — too vague
  • 1. Give the persona a body with limits.

Then the boy. Bread and jelly. Stuffed animals.
The father searching for a bedtime story.

Not just “a grieving father.” Someone who did these particular things with this particular child.

  • 2. Build relationships through small, specific details.
  • explain how much he loves his son — show what they did together

Possible weakness / risk: the familiar details—ball games, bedtime—could become a standard picture of fatherhood. But the boy slows down for his father. Care goes both ways.

3. Let the persona be more than one thing.

Strong enough to dig, struggling to dig. Caring for his son, also being cared for.
Give him contradictions
Give him different capacities in different situations.

Three pillars so far

  • A body with limits
  • Particular relationships
  • More than one role or capacity
