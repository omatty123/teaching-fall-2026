The Caribbean values

In Chapter VI, he says half a bit equals three pennies in England, and two bits equal one British shilling. He then sells gin for eight bits and glasses for two, making one dollar. That is how this chart gets ten bits to a dollar. This is his account’s arithmetic, not a rule for every place and date.

The Chapter V footnote values a pisterine at one shilling without naming the currency.

The freedom payment

The £40 British amount and £70 Montserrat amount describe this payment. This chart does not use their ratio to convert every other amount in the book.

Pound and guinea

The pound is a unit of money: 20 shillings. The guinea is a gold coin. It originally represented one pound, but its value changed as gold’s price shifted against silver. In 1717, its value was set at 21 shillings: one pound plus one shilling, or 5% more. 1717 proclamation The pictured one-pound sovereign came in 1817, after the book. The pictured penny is a later 1797 issue. Royal Mint Museum
