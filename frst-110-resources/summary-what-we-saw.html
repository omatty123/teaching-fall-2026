What we saw in the Summaries

Most people understood their chosen chapter or process. The misses were mostly form: length, the paperwork, and letting opinion slip in. All of it is fixable, and you can resubmit.

CABIN: five checks for your summary

C Complete
Include the essential claims, evidence, and steps of your chosen chapter or process.
A Accurate
Check names, numbers, sequence, and causes against Egan. Keep his claims distinct from your inferences.
B Brief
Stay within 500 words. Cut repetition without losing essential evidence.
I Independent
Give enough context for someone who has not read your chosen section.
N Neutral
Represent Egan’s account fairly. Keep your praise, blame, and proposed solutions separate.

What the strongest summaries did

The assignment allows a chapter or a process. Complete means complete within that chosen scope.

  1. Identified the book, chosen chapter or process, and main point early.

    A hook draws the reader in: “A canal built for ships also opened a route for invasive species.” Then name the book, your focus, and its main point. A hook is optional.

  2. Cited specific evidence accurately and made clear which claims came from Egan.
  3. Covered the key claims, evidence, and steps of the chosen chapter or process, including complications essential to explaining it.
  4. Reported what Egan says, not what they think of it.
  5. Explained how the chosen chapter or process fits the larger book.

The five most common fixes

Examples are made up, not from anyone’s paper.

    Opinion slipped in (N).Egan brilliantly proves we are destroying the lakes. → Egan argues that … [cite the actual supporting page]. Report the claim; save your view for the Synthesis.

    Over 500 words (B).Economize: make the background and scene-setting more concise while preserving their purpose. Tighten wording and remove repetition; retain the context, claims, and evidence the reader needs.

    No Honor Code line.I hereby reaffirm the Lawrence University Honor Code. The essay template (.docx) now has it; the earlier version omitted it.
  • 6
    Check citation coverage.Make each quotation and specific piece of evidence traceable to its page. The number of citations alone does not establish adequate coverage.
  • 5
    Works Cited missing or wrong.Egan, Dan. The Death and Life of the Great Lakes. W. W. Norton, 2017. Identify the book and the chapter or chapters containing your selected material, using your chosen citation style.
