What are the blues?

Delta blues · October 14, 16 and 19

A verse is twelve bars and three lines

Count four beats to a bar. A blues verse runs twelve bars. The words come in three lines, four bars each:

Abars 1–4 · the singer states a line
Abars 5–8 · the same line again
Bbars 9–12 · a new line answers it

Inside each line there is a second answer. The singer takes the first two bars or so, then stops. The piano or guitar answers in the rest. Call and response.

    The usual chords: I, IV, V

    Under the words, the band usually moves through three chords. Call the home chord I. IV is a step away; V pulls hardest back home. The common twelve-bar plan:

    Plain piano chords, one verse, with a click on each beat.

    Players change the plan all the time: an early IV in bar 2, a V in bar 12 to lead into the next verse. Listen for the turn to IV at bar 5 and the pull of V at bar 9. That is where the B line lands.

    Charley Patton bends it: “High Water Everywhere, Part 2”

    Patton starts in A A B, but his A lines already change on the repeat (marked A′). After three verses the form gives way: lines stop repeating, he talks to the listener, and the flood comes in pieces.
