Titles and first lines

Titles

  1. Name the thing.A stranger should guess your subject. A Continental Invasion, not Impending Doom.
  2. Promise a story.A person, a risk, a verb that hints at trouble. Tanner’s Gamble. How One Man Tried to Fix Lake Michigan.
  3. Steal the author’s best phrase.Lake Erie’s Kidney. Angry Enough to Put the Line Back.
  4. A label is not a title.Chapter 7 Summary is a file name.

First lines

  1. Start with something you can see.A thing, a place, a date, a number. In 1968 Lake Michigan shores were covered with rotting alewives.
  2. Small thing, huge effect.A single specimen small enough to hide in a boat’s bilge can cause problems that span an entire continent.
  3. Put the reader somewhere.Have you ever been fishing on the Great Lakes?
  4. Set up a turn.People overreact all the time. … and, unfortunately, they might be right.

Always

  • After the hook, get to the point. Within two or three sentences, name the book, the chapter, and what it’s about.
  • Cut the throat-clearing: It is no secret that, Throughout history, Since the dawn of time.
  • Write your first line last.

The test for both: could it head someone else’s paper? Then it isn’t yours yet.
