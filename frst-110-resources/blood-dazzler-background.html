Pickaninny

A racist term and caricature of Black children. A caricature is a representation that exaggerates selected features. This one reduces children to a racial stereotype, denying their individuality and making their suffering seem unimportant.

The word appears here for historical and literary analysis. The linked museum essay includes racist language and images of violence against children.

Historical background

The Jim Crow Museum documents this caricature in advertising, postcards, household objects, stage performances, and film. Common features included exaggerated eyes and mouths, ragged clothing, and depictions of children as dirty, foolish, or animal-like. Some images treated attacks on Black children as entertainment.

The museum traces one influential example through Topsy in Uncle Tom’s Cabin (1852). Stowe intended her suffering to expose slavery’s cruelty; minstrel adaptations turned the character’s neglect and hardship into comedy.

The museum uses the spelling picaninny; pickaninny and piccaninny are variants.

Read David Pilgrim, “The Picaninny Caricature” · Jim Crow Museum, Ferris State University

Bring the history back to the poem

Distinguish the poet, the poem’s speaker, and any voice the speaker quotes. A poem can reproduce racist language to expose it; identify what the surrounding lines do with the word.

  2. Identify a nearby detail that gives the child an individual voice, action, or experience.
  3. Explain how that detail supports, complicates, or challenges the stereotype described by the museum.

Saint Rita · impossible causes and the incorrupt body

Saint Rita of Cascia is the patron saint of impossible and desperate causes. A patron saint is a saint associated with particular people, occupations, or needs. In Catholic practice, people ask saints to intercede: to pray to God on their behalf. Source: National Shrine of Saint Rita of Cascia.

The irony of the nursing home’s name

St. Rita’s Nursing Home bears the name of a saint invoked when help seems impossible. In “34,” residents face catastrophe in a place intended to care for them. The name brings the expectation of aid into painful contrast with their deaths. This is an interpretive connection we can examine, not a documented statement of Smith’s intention.

Incorrupt · relic · reliquary

Incorrupt is the religious term used for a body believed to have escaped ordinary decay after death. Notre Dame’s account describes Rita’s body this way. A relic is a bodily remain or object venerated for its connection with a holy person; a reliquary is its container. Rita died in 1457. Her body is kept in an urn in the Basilica of Saint Rita in Cascia, Italy. Source: Notre Dame · Source: Basilica of Saint Rita.
