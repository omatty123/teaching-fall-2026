## Assigned readings

- Patricia Buckley Ebrey and Anne Walthall, *Pre-Modern East Asia: To 1800: A Cultural, Social, and Political History*, third edition, pp.93–96 (Tang exchange), 162–168 (Mongols), 176–182 (Goryeo). Interpretive explanations on the page are course synthesis, not primary testimony.
- E. Bretschneider, *Mediæval Researches from Eastern Asiatic Sources*, vol. I (1888), pp.37–39, letter issued in Chinggis Khan’s name, dated May 15, 1219. English translation is public domain. Reader PDF pp.2–4. Bretschneider’s suggested scribe is explicitly conjectural.
- David Morgan, *The Mongols*, assigned extract pp.73–83; Reader PDF pp.5–15. Modern secondary analysis. One 13-word quotation from p.78. The assigned scan’s cover does not establish an edition; no edition is inferred.
- Peter H. Lee and Wm. Theodore de Bary, eds., with Yongho Ch’oe and Hugh H. W. Kang, *Sources of Korean Tradition*, vol. I (Columbia University Press, 1997): p.201 editorial framing; pp.202–203 *Koryŏ sa* 103:23a–26a on Pak Sŏ and Kuju. Reader PDF p.17 contains printed pp.202–203. The translation is signed “HK AND ES” on p.203; those initials are retained without an unverified expansion. Two short quotations total 15 words. A later historical account is distinguished from a contemporaneous transcript.
- [November 5 Reader PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/171KJHYOyHKLDY5VKwyrjKUkDT27DdXZY/view). Original reading files and full copyrighted sources are not republished on this public page.

## Tripitaka Koreana

- [UNESCO World Heritage: Haeinsa](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/737/): thirteenth-century woodblocks, fifteenth-century storage buildings, defensive religious purpose, conservation design. Its 1237–1248 carving dates differ from Ebrey p.176’s 1237–1251; the class page records both.
- [UNESCO Memory of the World](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/printing-woodblocks-tripitaka-koreana-and-miscellaneous-buddhist-scriptures): collection in classical Chinese, 81,258 blocks, commissioned by Goryeo.
- Photograph reused from the course artwork collection: [Tripitaka Koreana.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tripitaka_Koreana.jpg), Arian Zwegers, derivative by MrPanyGoff, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/). Public asset `images/artwork/15-tripitaka.jpg`; no new crop or content edits. A present-day photograph, not an image of the original carving workshop.
