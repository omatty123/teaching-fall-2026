## Text evidence

- Assigned Oct13 reader: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DgdxWmNkDr22Sze26MjL-juHOmhtmLPF/view . Existing excerpts retained; builder verifies against local readers/Ebrey corpus. New paragraphs are synthesis, not translated quotations.
- SCT: Wm. Theodore de Bary and Irene Bloom, eds., Sources of Chinese Tradition, vol.1, 2nd ed., pp.416–429. Source edition controls quoted English. Page lists editors rather than inventing an unverified individual translator credit.
- SKT: Peter H. Lee and Wm. Theodore de Bary, eds., Sources of Korean Tradition, vol.1 (1997), pp.41–56. Kakhun's Haedong kosung chon (1215) is the source of the Won'gwang and conversion biographies; Peter H. Lee's complete translation corroborates the course excerpt.
- Petition: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0002050 ; original wording does not survive; commissioned608, request associated with611. https://db.history.go.kr/item/compareViewer.do?levelId=sg_004r_0060_0350 is Samguk sagi's608 entry. The quoted SKT45 words are a reported reply to the king, not the petition.
- Huiyuan: https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Lushan_Huiyuan ; 334–416, Mount Lu, essay404, Huan Xuan.
- Mouzi attribution: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouzi_Lihuolun ; late-Han narrative setting distinguished from debated authorship/date.
- Wang's precise lifespan omitted: Ebrey gives307–365, while museum convention often gives303–361. Both place him in fourth-century Eastern Jin.
